A2-Sign language learning game!

Ever felt like you wanted to learn sign language but didn't feel that fun? Here is a game, mirror, so you can get started!

Notes

Try to smile to see flowers!

How to use it

  • Sign a letter and it floats above your hand. Read: 123 switches to numbers 0 to 9.
  • What you sign is written along the bottom: hold a sign for a moment to add it, and lower your hand for a second to start a new word. Press S to save that record, and the Notes button (bottom right) downloads your saved records as a .txt file.
  • Fix pauses everything so you can teach it how your hand makes a letter it gets wrong.
  • Chart shows the ASL alphabet, and Game starts the levels, which never end after level 3.

How accurate is the AI?

The sign reading is a guess, and it gets things wrong. In practice mode, the letter model got about 81% of letters right on a sample of its own training data (its author reports 97%). The game's letter rules are simpler, and less accurate than that. Letters that look alike, like M, N, A, S, T and E, get mixed up most, and J and Z are hard because they move. Lighting, how far you are from the camera, and which hand you use all change the results. If it keeps reading a letter wrong, press Fix and teach it how your hand makes it. The real ASL signs in the demo box are machine-translated, so treat them as a guide, not a teacher.

Sources

  • p5.js and p5.sound, for drawing and sound.
  • ml5.js: body segmentation, hand tracking (handPose, which uses MediaPipe Hands) and the neural network for signs you teach it. Body segmentation is based on the ml5.js example bodySegmentation-mask-person.
  • Smile tracking: face-api.js by Vincent Mühler, loaded through @vladmandic/face-api (an updated version that works alongside ml5).
  • Practice mode letters: the trained model from ASL-Recognition-System by Muhib Mehdi (MIT license), its weights run in JavaScript.
  • Game letters: rules adapted from Sign_Language_Detection by gurkirat20.
  • Real ASL sign animations: sign.mt, played with pose-viewer.
  • ASL alphabet chart (the ABC button): public domain, from Wikimedia Commons.
  • Hand buttons: the "hand" icon by loading.io, CC-BY, loading.io/icon/ckjo44, recoloured.
  • Endless levels: inspired by the "Random Numbers" game in Gesture Academy by Narottam04 (MIT license).
  • Sound effects: "level up" by universfield, "magical sparkle whoosh" by djartmusic and "fairy dust magical chime" by emand_edroff, from Pixabay.
